# Licences, not APIs, decide Superfinancials' daily feeds

Superfinancials can fill most of its daily gaps at no cost, but the real limit is the licence, not the data. Free, commercially reusable sources cover the platform's weakest areas: **EIA API v2** for US energy; **SMARD/Energy-Charts** for European power (CC BY 4.0); **Eurostat, BLS and ONS** release calendars; **SEC Form C and Companies House SH01 streams** for private rounds; **BODACC, Brønnøysund, French SIRENE and Estonia's daily bulk files** for registries; and **DMO Nigeria's daily Eurobond XLSX**, which is the only free official African sovereign Eurobond series found. The binding constraint is redistribution rights. **FRED's API terms forbid storing, caching or archiving FRED content**, and that directly conflicts with how the platform uses FRED today. **IMF data (CPI, PCPS commodity prices, PortWatch) needs written permission from copyright@imf.org before commercial reuse.** Nigeria's interbank fixings and secondary yields are a paid FMDQ product at roughly **₦13m–₦34m a year**. The benchmark commodity and freight prices (Platts, Argus, LME, ICE, Baltic Exchange) have no legal free daily feed. **X ingestion is legal and affordable only through the official pay-per-use API**: about $0.005 per post read, so roughly **$150–$600/month for ~200 accounts**. Scraping is banned and backed by liquidated damages, and the posts are best treated as an alerting layer, not a store of record. One caution applies to everything below. **Nothing in this report was verified live:** the research sandbox's proxy blocked or failed DNS for every provider host tried, so every endpoint, field name and rate limit comes from official-page search snippets, open-source code or third-party documentation. Each one must be smoke-tested from Vercel before anyone builds on it.

## Three licence flags outrank every endpoint choice

**FRED is the most urgent problem because the platform already depends on it.** The FRED API Terms of Use prohibit "using the API in connection with storing, caching, or archiving any portion of FRED Content", and also prohibit providing stored content to third parties. Many series belong to third parties that FRED "cannot give you such permission" to reuse, and those series are flagged "Copyright" in their notes ([FRED API Terms](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/terms_of_use.html); [FRED legal](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/legal)). The README routes US government yields through FRED, so a cron that writes FRED values to a database breaches those terms as written. The fix is to source each series from the agency that produces it. US Treasury FiscalData is already connected. The BLS and BEA APIs are US public domain. EIA covers energy. OECD covers international series that FRED only re-hosts, for example `OECDLRHUTTTTSTSAM` ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/OECDLRHUTTTTSTSAQ)). FRED should then be kept for live, non-stored lookups only. That covers regional Fed surveys such as Empire State `GACDISA066MSFRBNY` and Philly Fed `GACDFSA066MSFRBPHI`, which have no obvious substitute ([FRED Empire](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GACDISA066MSFRBNY)). IMF-sourced FRED series carry an explicit copyright label ([FRED IMF graph](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=Ho1d)), so pulling them through FRED does not avoid the IMF problem.

**The IMF is the second flag, and it gates the single best source for African macro data.** The IMF's new SDMX 3.0 API covers national CPI for 187 countries monthly, as well as quarterly GDP, interest rates, reserves and FX. Yet the IMF's terms say "for any potential commercial reuse of IMF Data, please email copyright@imf.org to request permission", and the IMF runs a commercial licensing program with a rate card ([IMF Copyright and Usage](https://www.imf.org/en/About/copyright-and-terms); [IMF Commercial Licensing](https://bookstore.imf.org/licensing)). Primary Commodity Prices sit under the IMF's special terms. They require the same permission, and anyone selling them must tell buyers the data is free from the IMF ([IMF terms](https://www.imf.org/en/about/copyright-and-terms/index.md)). PortWatch, the best free freight proxy, is reportedly under "imf-special-terms-viii" ([statisticsoftheworld](https://statisticsoftheworld.com/series/PORTWATCH.CHOKEPOINT.TRANSITS.D)). The permission email should go out this week. Until a reply arrives, IMF-derived numbers should sit behind a feature flag.

**The third flag covers the sources that are clean, which is most official non-US data.** OECD content published since 1 July 2024 defaults to CC BY 4.0 ([OECD open-by-default](https://oecd.org/en/about/oecd-open-by-default-policy.html)). Eurostat authorises commercial reuse with acknowledgement ([Eurostat copyright](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice)). BIS use is "unrestricted", provided the use implies no endorsement and a commercial product adds "no additional charge to subscribers" for the BIS data ([BIS legal](https://data.bis.org/help/legal)); that clause deserves a pricing-page review, since a paywalled "macro" tier could breach it. DBnomics adds no rights of its own: its data carries "the same license and terms of use as its original source provider" ([DBnomics legal](https://docs.db.nomics.world/legal-terms/)). Trading Economics allows redistribution only for enterprise clients ([TE API](https://tradingeconomics.com/analytics/api.aspx)).

The pattern is consistent across the continent. **Nigeria's free official data covers primary-market auction results and the Eurobond curve, while everything that trades in the secondary market belongs to FMDQ.** FMDQ's 2026 framework has separate distribution and commercial-analytical licences and was revised at least four times this year ([Feb](https://fmdqgroup.com/exchange/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FMDQ-Exchange-2026-Fees-and-Dues-Framework.pdf); [Jul](https://fmdqgroup.com/exchange/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/FMDQ-Exchange-2026-Fees-and-Dues-Framework.pdf)). Scraping FMDQ numbers into a commercial app is therefore high-risk. South Africa needs one schema change soon: **JIBAR ends after its final publication on 31 December 2026**, with the credit adjustment spread locked as at 3 December 2025 ([Creamer Media](https://newsletter.en.creamermedia.com/article/sarb-announces-jibar-to-cease-at-end-of-2026-2025-12-03)). ZARONIA has been published daily since November 2023, with history back to January 2016 ([CBN.co.za](https://cbn.co.za/industry-news/business-advisory-financial-services-news/the-end-of-jibar-and-what-zaronia-means-for-sa/)), so the platform should build ZARONIA now and mark JIBAR end-of-life. BCEAO's EDEN portal has no simple machine endpoint at all ([DBnomics issue](https://git.nomics.world/dbnomics-fetchers/management/-/issues/13)).

For **equities and indices**, the connected African APIs already cover the listed side; none was confirmed to carry rates. Mansa Markets claims 21 exchanges, with a free tier of 100 requests/day and US$50/month for 10,000 requests/day, refreshing every 30 minutes in market hours ([Mansa pricing](https://www.mansamarkets.com/pricing)). One caveat: the repo points at `mansaapi.com`, and it is unconfirmed whether that is the same company. MyStocks Africa publishes machine-readable partner docs ([raw docs](https://mystocks.africa/partners/docs/raw/product-market-data)). EODHD's `GBOND` exchange lists `KE10Y.GBOND`, but its snapshot was dated December 2024, so the series may be stale ([EODHD](https://eodhd.com/financial-summary/KE10Y.GBOND)). The fastest way to settle African yield coverage is one call with the existing key, `GET https://eodhd.com/api/exchange-symbol-list/GBOND?api_token=…&fmt=json`, filtered for ZA, NG, KE, EG, GH and MA. That URL is the standard EODHD pattern, not tested here.

**Sovereign ratings have no free, licence-clean daily feed.** SEC Rule 17g-7(b) requires each NRSRO to publish rating histories as XBRL ([SEC OCR](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/office-credit-ratings/disclosure-of-credit-rating-histories)), but the files are published on each agency's own site under its own terms, and they lag. ESMA's European Rating Platform is free to view and includes sovereign research ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/82409)), but ESMA itself acknowledged that it lacks machine-readable bulk download ([ESMA 2020](https://www.esma.europa.eu/system/files/consultations/2020/07/credit_ratings_questionnaire_a.pdf)). The defensible approach is a hand-curated table of rating actions recorded as facts (agency, sovereign, rating, outlook, date), each linked to the agency's press release. For Eurobonds outside Nigeria, Cbonds starts at about US$450/month, with redistribution priced separately ([Datarade](https://datarade.ai/data-products/market-data-api-global-coverage-350-pricing-sources-cbonds)).

## Free commodity data is monthly; daily benchmarks are proprietary

The commodity picture splits cleanly in two. **US energy and European power have excellent free daily sources; almost every other benchmark on the wish list is daily only behind a paid licence.** Neither EODHD nor FMP could be confirmed for TTF, JKM, Newcastle coal, iron ore, lithium, CPO, urea/DAP, EUA, German power, BDI or TD3C. FMP's `/stable/commodities-list` and `/stable/quote?symbol=GCUSD` cover the futures-style basics ([FMP docs](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/developer/docs/stable/commodities-list)). EODHD's commodity endpoint is daily for WTI, Brent and Henry Hub but "generally monthly only" for metals ([EODHD](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis/commodities-api/)), and its own pages conflict on which plans include it.

Pull EIA daily history from the API, not the spreadsheets. A DataHub note reports that EIA's daily spot-price workbooks dropped hundreds of historical observations in September 2026 ([DataHub](https://datahub.io/datapressr/oil-prices)). For the "proprietary to avoid" list, the notes rule out Platts, Argus, LME, ICE, CME (beyond delayed display), the Baltic Exchange, Bloomberg (including BCOM) and Investing.com-style scraping. Resellers such as commodities-api.com offer TTF and aluminium prices, but their upstream licensing is unclear ([commodities-api](https://commodities-api.com/blog/access-dutch-ttf-gas-monthly-tfmi-historical-rates-from-this-api)). In Nigeria, the CBN's own PMI (56.4 in February 2026, per [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/?p=2798410)) is a legal alternative to the S&P Stanbic IBTC series. One commercial site claims the OECD CLI dataflow was retired ([convextrade](https://convextrade.com/metrics/oecd-cli-g7)), but working 2025–26 code contradicts it ([OpenAlice](https://github.com/TraderAlice/OpenAlice)).

## The OECD 500 is a throttling problem, and IMF fills the African macro gap

The platform's failing OECD CPI query is very likely not malformed. Its key, `USA.M.N.CPI.PA._T.N.GY` against `DSD_PRICES@DF_PRICES_ALL`, matches the 8-dimension order and is used successfully in other 2025–26 projects ([macro-pipeline](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ECRSSREW22E/macro-pipeline/HEAD/docs/API_SOURCES.md); [puremacro](https://github.com/jalonso1979/puremacro/blob/HEAD/puremacro/fetch/oecd_energy.py)). **sdmx.oecd.org answers HTTP 500 when overloaded and rate-limits bursts** ([vistamark](https://github.com/MRRVista/vistamark-ria-intel)). It also returns 403 to the default `Python-urllib` User-Agent ([basedosdados](https://github.com/basedosdados/pipelines)), and big queries can return 404s that clear on retry ([econdatalibrary](https://github.com/elkassabgi/econdatalibrary)). OECD's own pages disagree on the hourly limit: one says 20 queries/minute and 20 downloads/hour, the French version and FAQ say 60 downloads/hour ([OECD best practices](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2024/11/Api-best-practices-and-recommendations.html); [FAQ](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2024/09/OECD-DE-FAQ.html)). The fix has four parts:

- Replace the 37-country `+` list with an empty REF_AREA wildcard and an empty version segment: `https://sdmx.oecd.org/public/rest/data/OECD.SDD.TPS,DSD_PRICES@DF_PRICES_ALL,/.M.N.CPI.PA._T.N.GY?startPeriod=2016-01&format=csvfile`. Then map countries on the client.
- Send a browser-like User-Agent.
- Retry with backoff on 429, 500 and 503.
- Never run OECD calls in parallel.

Expect China, India, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, Argentina, South Africa and Brazil to be thin or missing from this flow. The same approach works for monthly unemployment (`DSD_LFS@DF_IALFS_UNE_M`, key `.UNE_LF_M.PT_LF_SUB._Z.Y._T.Y_GE15._Z.M`), quarterly GDP (`DSD_NAMAIN1@DF_QNA_EXPENDITURE_GROWTH_OECD`) and the CLI ([quarcs-lab](https://github.com/quarcs-lab/macro102-site); [FinanceToolkit](https://github.com/JerBouma/FinanceToolkit)).

Under these terms, IMF CPI is the only uniform monthly CPI source for Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, Egypt, Morocco, Côte d'Ivoire and Zambia. Nigeria's NBS has a download-only portal ([NBS](https://nigerianstat.gov.ng/)), KNBS has no confirmed API ([KNBS](https://www.knbs.or.ke/knbs-data/)), and SARB still has no easy programmatic interface ([sarbR](https://github.com/HanjoStudy/sarbR)). **IMF permission therefore decides the platform's African macro coverage.** Without it, the fallbacks are BIS `WS_LONG_CPI` where it has coverage, Ghana's PX-Web, and PDF parsing of national releases.

**Calendars are machine-readable for statistics offices and almost never for central banks.** The usable feeds are:

- Eurostat's euro-indicators ICS at `ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/RELEASE_CALENDAR/calendar_EN.ics`, updated at 11:00 and 23:00 with a status per release ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/news/internet-calendar));
- BLS at `www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/bls.ics` ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm));
- the keyless but beta ONS `api.beta.ons.gov.uk/v1/search/releases` ([ONS](https://developer.ons.gov.uk/search/search-releases/)).

BEA publishes only HTML schedules ([BEA](https://www.bea.gov/news/schedule/next-year)). Central banks, from the BoE ([MPC dates](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy/upcoming-mpc-dates)) to the BoG ([2026 dates](https://www.bog.gov.gh/news/mpc-meeting-dates-for-2026/)), CBK ([notice](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/2026/05/11/next-mpc-meeting-3/)) and CBN ([PDF](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2025/CCD/Notice%20of%20MPC%20Meeting.pdf)), post dates as HTML or PDF. Third-party ICS feeds were stale as of mid-2026 and inconsistent with each other ([smartcalendars](https://smartcalendars.ai/en/feeds/fed-fomc-meeting-calendar); [centralbank.watch](https://centralbank.watch/tools/calendar/)). The robust design is a curated, source-cited `central_bank_meetings` table, plus a weekly job that hashes each official page and alerts when it changes.

## Private-market data is free only where governments compel disclosure

**No African or EM deal tracker offers a feed that a commercial app may legally ingest and redisplay.** Africa: The Big Deal sells an *individual-licence* Excel file, updated monthly ([Gumroad](https://thebigdeal.gumroad.com/l/bQSRD/d9esvpe)), and that licence almost certainly does not cover a multi-user app. Briter bars resale, redistribution and building competing products without a separate licence ([Briter data policy](https://briter.co/data-policy)). Crunchbase reportedly stopped issuing free Basic keys, though this is confirmed only by third parties ([dev.to](https://dev.to/agenthustler/crunchbase-api-in-2026-free-tier-gone-what-startup-data-hunters-do-now-1177)). Dealroom's API is custom-priced ([Dealroom pricing](https://www.dealroom.co/pricing/)). Partech's annual report, which put **African tech funding at $4.1B in 2025, up 25%** ([allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202601250064.html)), is context, not a feed.

The free daily sources are regulatory filings, and many African rounds pass through them, because start-ups are often held through Delaware, UK or Mauritius parents.

SH01s need a classification heuristic, because many are option exercises or small top-ups. Form PF is not public: the SEC releases only aggregates, such as about **$25.5T in total private-fund GAV in 2025 Q1** ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/files/investment/private-funds-statistics-2025-q1.pdf)). For coverage beyond filings, the practical route is **LLM extraction from the news APIs already connected** (Marketaux, NewsAPI.ai, Currents), which is effectively how the trackers compile their data. Each extracted record should hold company, country, amount, currency, round, instrument, lead investors, date and source URL. Records should be deduplicated on company plus amount within ±10% over a 30-day window, and every one should be flagged "reported (news)" or "filed (regulatory)". Big Deal's $100K+ threshold ([about](https://thebigdeal.substack.com/about)) and Partech's equity-over-$200K rule ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2666997)) show why the instrument and threshold rules must be explicit. The terms of those three news APIs on storing derived facts were not checked and need review.

## Registries and alt data: Europe is open, Africa's registries are gated

The EU high-value-datasets regime explains why several European registries now offer free APIs. The best additions are listed below.

- **Norway's Brønnøysund:** `data.brreg.no/enhetsregisteret/api/enheter/{orgnr}`, keyless, NLOD 2.0 per a secondary source ([API docs](https://data.brreg.no/enhetsregisteret/api/dokumentasjon/en/index.html); [tidybrreg](https://sondreskarsten.r-universe.dev/tidybrreg/doc/business-data.html)).
- **France's recherche-entreprises:** keyless, e.g. `recherche-entreprises.api.gouv.fr/search?q=danone&per_page=5`, with SIRENE under Licence Ouverte ([guide](https://dev.to/openregistry/france-sirene-rne-post-canonicalisation-api-guide-1j24)).
- **Estonia:** free daily XML/JSON bulk files once the terms are accepted ([RIK](https://abiinfo.rik.ee/en/e-business-register-queries/open-data-e-business-register)). Beneficial-owner data may have stopped being open in July 2026 ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/datasets/ee_ariregister)).
- **Finland PRH, Czech ARES and Poland KRS:** strong candidates on prior knowledge, but none was verified.
- **KVK (Netherlands):** paid, at about €6.40/month plus €0.02 per query ([KVK](https://developers.kvk.nl/faq/billing)).
- **South Africa's CIPC and Nigeria's CAC:** no open API. Access goes through approval or a NIBSS partnership ([companydata](https://companydata.com/cipc-api/); [RegTech Africa](https://regtechafrica.com/?p=65082)).
- **OpenCorporates:** free keys are ODbL share-alike, so commercial use needs paid keys ([terms](https://opencorporates.com/legal-information/self-service-api-terms-of-service/)).

For identifiers, GLEIF (connected) and OpenFIGI are free. OpenFIGI's mapping allows about 25 req/min anonymously and about 250 req/min with a free key, though sources conflict ([openfigi-rs](https://docs.rs/crate/openfigi-rs/0.3.3)); the request is `POST api.openfigi.com/v3/mapping` with `[{"idType":"ID_ISIN","idValue":"US0378331005"}]`. **OpenSanctions is not free for commercial use.** Its bulk data is CC BY-NC 4.0, and an external-facing product would likely need the reseller/OEM tier ([OpenSanctions licensing](https://www.opensanctions.org/sponsor)).

Among alternative data sources, the verified free options are:

- **PatentsView**, with a free non-expiring key and 45 requests/minute ([PatentSearch API](https://search.patentsview.org/docs/docs/Search%20API/SearchAPIReference));
- **EPO OPS**, free up to 4 GB a week ([EPO fair use](https://www.epo.org/en/service-support/ordering/fair-use));
- **PortWatch**, subject to the IMF caveat;
- **GDELT DOC 2.0**, keyless, with 15-minute updates over a rolling 3-month window, at about 1 request per 5 seconds ([GDELT](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/gdelt-doc-2-0-api-debuts/)). Its commercial-use permission is quoted only second-hand ([jwatte](https://jwatte.com/blog/gdelt-open-data-kalev-leetaru/)).

Wikimedia pageviews, USAspending, EU TED, UN Comtrade and NASA Black Marble are plausible daily panels from prior knowledge, but their terms were not fetched this session. The sources to avoid or defer are:

- **Google Trends:** the API is still invite-only alpha ([Google](https://developers.google.cn/search/blog/2025/07/trends-api)).
- **OpenSky:** needs a commercial agreement ([OpenSky](https://openskynetwork.github.io/opensky-api)).
- **Google News RSS:** for personal, non-commercial use only ([Google News terms](https://www.google.com/intl/en_us/terms_google_news.html)).

## X is a paid alerting layer, not a database

**X ingestion is viable and legal for a commercial app in late 2026, but only through the official API, and only for discovery.** Pricing is now pay-per-use: **$0.005 per post read and $0.010 per user read**, deduplicated within each 24-hour UTC window ([X pricing](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing)). The monthly cap is 2 million post reads per X's docs, or 3 million per some 2026 sources ([X post cap](https://docs.x.com/x-api/fundamentals/post-cap); [Outstand](https://www.outstand.so/blog/x-api-pricing)). New developers get no free tier, and the legacy $200 Basic and $5,000 Pro plans are closed to newcomers ([PostProxy](https://postproxy.dev/blog/x-api-pricing-2026/)). For about 200 accounts that produce 1,000–4,000 posts a day, polling `GET /2/users/:id/tweets` with `since_id` comes to roughly **$150–$600/month**. That figure is an estimate built on assumed posting volumes, and it sits well under the cap.

The terms constrain the product more than the price does:

- X's Developer Guidelines prohibit AI/ML training "except for Grok" ([X guidelines](https://docs.x.com/developer-guidelines)).
- After June 2025 the Developer Agreement bars using X content to train foundation models ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/05/x-changes-its-terms-to-bar-training-of-ai-models-using-its-content)).
- Redistribution is capped at 1.5M post IDs per 30 days per entity and 50,000 hydrated posts per recipient per day.
- The guidelines state that "non-API access is prohibited".
- The January 2026 user terms keep liquidated damages of **$15,000 per million posts** scraped in a 24-hour period ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/x-expands-content-to-ai-prompts-outputs-in-2026-terms-update/)).

*X v. Bright Data* gave some comfort to scrapers of logged-out data in 2024 ([Skadden](https://skadden.com/insights/publications/2024/05/district-court-adopts-broad-view)). It does not protect a company that holds an X developer account. Display rules, deletion-compliance obligations and per-endpoint rate limits could not be read this session.

Strategically, X adds speed and little else for official sources. The Fed, BLS, ECB and others publish RSS feeds ([Fed feeds](https://federalreserve.gov/feeds); [BLS feeds](https://www.bls.gov/feed)), and the African central banks post the same figures on their websites. The highest-value X use is **funding-round discovery** from founders, VCs and trackers such as Africa: The Big Deal, whose monthly tallies (for example **$174m across 26 deals in January 2026**, per [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/02/09/african-startups-raise-174m-in-january-2026-deals-hit-record-low/)) get picked up by the press. The candidate handle list has not been verified, and confirming it costs about $0.01 per handle. Its official names are @cenbank, @CBKKenya, @SAReserveBank, @federalreserve, @ecb, @bankofengland, @nbs_nigeria, @StatsSA, @DMONigeria, @NGXGroup, @BLS_gov, @BEA_News and @EIAgov. Media accounts (@Nairametrics, @Proshare, @BusinessDayNg) and funding trackers should be tagged `source_type=secondary`. The safe pattern is to store post IDs and the figures confirmed against the linked primary page, never post text. Bluesky's open API is a free complement for the ECB, which joined in January 2025 ([RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2025/0113/1490550-european-central-bank-joins-bluesky-rival-to-musks-x)).

## Conclusion

The research changes what "data coverage" means for Superfinancials. Wiring more APIs adds little now. The bigger gain is **provenance and licence hygiene**: one data source the platform relies on (FRED) prohibits the storage pattern in use, and the best African macro source (IMF) is legally gated. The differentiating African content, meanwhile, comes from unglamorous document watchers, not vendor APIs. That pushes engineering towards a shared "poll, diff, parse, attribute" framework for PDFs, XLSX files and HTML pages. The same framework serves DMO, CBN, CBK, BoG, pension-fund tables and central-bank calendars, so its value compounds across at least five of the ranked items.

The second implication is commercial. The few things that would make the platform look like a terminal for Nigeria (FMDQ fixings), for commodity benchmarks (PRA and exchange prices) and for African deal flow (tracker licences) are all paid. Their costs are bounded and known. The free stack can carry a credible launch with honest "monthly" and "reported" labels. Paid licences can then be bought one at a time where usage proves demand, starting with whichever of FMDQ or X credits the first users actually ask for.
